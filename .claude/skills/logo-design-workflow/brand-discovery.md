# Brand Discovery Skill

**Agent:** 🎯 Brand Specialist
**Phases:** Discover + Define (Double Diamond)
**Purpose:** Create strategic foundation before any visual design work begins

---

## Skill Overview

This skill ensures logo design starts with strategic clarity, not aesthetic guessing. Before any visuals are created, we establish:
- Who the brand is for
- What it stands for
- How it should feel
- What makes it distinct

**This is NOT one-shot generation.** It's a structured discovery process with stakeholder checkpoints.

---

## Phase 1: DISCOVER (Divergent Research)

### 1.1 Discovery Brief Intake

**Gather foundational information:**

```markdown
## Discovery Brief

### Business Context
- **Company/Project Name:**
- **Industry/Category:**
- **Core Offering:** What do you sell/provide?
- **Target Audience:** Who are your primary users/customers?
- **Key Differentiator:** What makes you different from competitors?

### Brand Aspirations
- **Brand Personality (3-5 adjectives):**
- **Emotional Response:** How should people feel when they see your brand?
- **Brands You Admire:** (not to copy, but for reference)
- **Brands to Avoid Resembling:**

### Practical Requirements
- **Primary Use Cases:** Where will the logo appear most? (web, mobile, print, signage)
- **Technical Constraints:** Any specific requirements? (must work at small sizes, etc.)
- **Timeline:**
- **Existing Assets:** Any current branding to evolve from?
```

### 1.2 Competitive Landscape Analysis

**Research and document:**

1. **Direct Competitors** (3-5)
   - Logo style (wordmark, symbol, combination)
   - Color palette
   - Typography approach
   - Overall positioning

2. **Adjacent/Aspirational Brands** (2-3)
   - What do they do well?
   - What elements resonate?

3. **Industry Visual Patterns**
   - Common clichés to avoid
   - Overused color schemes
   - Generic symbol tropes

**Output:** Competitive landscape summary with visual patterns to embrace/avoid

### 1.3 Audience Understanding

**Define primary audience segments:**

```markdown
## Audience Segment: [Name]

### Demographics
- Age range:
- Location:
- Profession/Role:

### Psychographics
- Values:
- Pain points:
- Aspirations:

### Visual Preferences
- Design sensibilities (modern/traditional, minimal/ornate)
- Platforms they use (where they'll encounter the brand)
- Cultural context (references they'll understand)
```

## Phase 2: DEFINE (Convergent Positioning)

### 2.1 Brand Archetype Identification

**Select 1-2 primary archetypes** (based on Jung/Mark & Pearson):

| Archetype | Core Desire | Brand Promise | Visual Language |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Innocent** | Safety | Life can be simple | Soft, clean, optimistic |
| **Explorer** | Freedom | Don't fence me in | Rugged, natural, adventurous |
| **Sage** | Truth | Knowledge is power | Refined, intellectual, timeless |
| **Hero** | Mastery | Where there's a will | Bold, strong, dynamic |
| **Outlaw** | Liberation | Rules are made to be broken | Edgy, provocative, dark |
| **Magician** | Power | Make dreams reality | Mystical, transformative |
| **Regular Guy** | Belonging | Everyone matters | Approachable, honest, practical |
| **Lover** | Intimacy | You're worth it | Sensual, luxurious, warm |
| **Jester** | Enjoyment | Life is fun | Playful, bright, irreverent |
| **Caregiver** | Service | Love your neighbor | Nurturing, supportive, soft |
| **Creator** | Innovation | If it can be imagined | Expressive, artistic, original |
| **Ruler** | Control | Power isn't everything | Premium, authoritative, structured |

**Document selection with rationale:**
```markdown
## Brand Archetype

**Primary:** [Archetype] - Because...
**Secondary:** [Archetype] - Because...

### Archetype Expression
- **Voice:** How the brand speaks
- **Visuals:** What the brand looks like
- **Experience:** How the brand feels
```

### 2.2 Positioning Statement

**Craft a clear positioning statement:**

```markdown
## Positioning Statement

For [target audience]
Who [need/opportunity]
[Brand name] is a [category]
That [key benefit/differentiator]
Unlike [competitors]
We [unique value proposition]
```

### 2.4 Creative Direction Moodboard

**Curate visual references for:**

1. **Color Direction**
   - 2-3 palette options with rationale
   - Emotional associations
   - Industry context (stand out vs. fit in)

2. **Typography Direction**
   - Serif vs. sans-serif rationale
   - Weight and presence (bold/light, commanding/subtle)
   - Personality expression

3. **Symbol/Mark Direction**
   - Abstract vs. literal
   - Geometric vs. organic
   - Complex vs. simple

4. **Overall Aesthetic**
   - Reference images that capture the feeling
   - NOT logos to copy, but mood/vibe references
   - Cultural and temporal context

**Moodboard Presentation Format:**
```markdown
## Creative Direction: [Direction Name]

### The Feeling
[2-3 sentences describing the emotional territory]

### Color Palette Direction
- Primary direction: [description + hex examples]
- Rationale: [why this feels right for the brand]

### Typography Direction
- Style: [serif/sans-serif/display]
- Character: [friendly, authoritative, modern, classic]
- References: [font families that capture this]

### Symbol/Mark Direction
- Approach: [abstract/literal/typographic]
- Form language: [geometric/organic/hybrid]
- Complexity: [minimal/moderate/detailed]

### Reference Images
[Curated images that capture the mood - not logos]

### What This Direction Achieves
- [Benefit 1]
- [Benefit 2]
- [Benefit 3]

### Potential Risks
- [Risk 1 and mitigation]
- [Risk 2 and mitigation]
```

## Checkpoint: Strategic Foundation Approval

**Before proceeding to visual exploration, confirm:**

- [ ] Discovery brief is complete and accurate
- [ ] Competitive landscape is understood
- [ ] Target audience is clearly defined
- [ ] Brand archetype(s) selected and justified
- [ ] Positioning statement approved
- [ ] Creative direction(s) presented
- [ ] Direction(s) to explore selected

**Approval Required:** Stakeholder sign-off before 🎨 Graphic Designer begins visual work

## Handoff to Graphic Designer

**Package for 🎨 Graphic Designer:**

```markdown
## Visual Exploration Brief

### Strategic Foundation
- **Brand:** [name]
- **Archetype:** [primary + secondary]
- **Positioning:** [one-liner]
- **Key Attributes:** [3-5 words]

### Approved Creative Direction
[Selected direction details]

### Exploration Parameters
- **Concepts Requested:** 3-5 distinct directions
- **Focus Areas:** [symbol, wordmark, combination, etc.]
- **Must-Haves:** [any non-negotiables]
- **Avoid:** [competitor clichés, specific styles]

### Success Criteria
The logo should:
- [ ] Express [archetype] personality
- [ ] Appeal to [target audience]
- [ ] Stand out from [competitive context]
- [ ] Work at [required sizes/applications]
- [ ] Feel [key emotional attributes]
```

## Quality Gates

### Discovery Phase Quality
- [ ] Stakeholder input captured, not assumed
- [ ] Competitor research is current and comprehensive
- [ ] Audience definition is specific, not generic
- [ ] Research informs strategy, not just documents it

### Define Phase Quality
- [ ] Archetype selection has clear rationale
- [ ] Positioning is differentiated, not generic
- [ ] Creative direction is inspiring, not prescriptive
- [ ] Multiple directions offered for exploration
- [ ] Direction includes both "do" and "don't"

### Handoff Quality
- [ ] Graphic Designer has everything needed to begin
- [ ] No ambiguity in strategic direction
- [ ] Success criteria are measurable
- [ ] Stakeholder expectations are set

---

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

**Discovery:**
- Skipping stakeholder input and assuming
- Surface-level competitor research
- Generic audience descriptions ("millennials who like quality")
- Rushing to visual preferences before strategic clarity

**Define:**
- Choosing archetype because it "sounds cool"
- Positioning that could apply to any competitor
- Creative direction that's too prescriptive (limits exploration)
- Creative direction that's too vague (no guidance)

**Handoff:**
- Incomplete strategic context for visual team
- Unstated assumptions about style preferences
- No checkpoint gate (visual work starts without approval)
